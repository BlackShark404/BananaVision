# Acceptance Testing

Acceptance testing evaluated the usability of BananaVision through the ten-item System Usability Scale (SUS), as specified in the approved methodology. The evaluation focused on the participants' experience of using the kiosk terminal and web dashboard for batch-scanning and management activities. Respondent profiles and optional comments provided context for the usability results.

Five evaluators from one wholesale banana distribution enterprise participated in the evaluation. Two participants, representing 40% of the respondents, were assigned Administrator roles for web dashboard and management activities. Three participants, representing 60%, were assigned Operator roles for kiosk terminal and batch-scanning activities. All five completed the ten SUS items, providing 50 responses for analysis.

The analysis followed the approved SUS scope. Four additional reliability items in the response workbook were excluded from the formal analysis and SUS score. The approved questionnaire wording and the original survey records were preserved.

## Results and Discussion

### Questionnaire Results: System Usability Scale

Responses were analyzed using the standard five-point agreement coding: 1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, and 5 = Strongly Agree. Item means and sample standard deviations (SD) were calculated to summarize the response patterns.

SUS includes five positively worded items and five negatively worded items. For odd-numbered items, the response minus 1 gives the score contribution. For even-numbered items, 5 minus the response gives the contribution. The ten contributions are added and multiplied by 2.5 to obtain a respondent's SUS score from 0 to 100. The overall result is the mean of the five respondent scores. [Brooke (1996)](https://hci-studies.org/methods-and-measures/downloads/SUS_Brooke1996.pdf)

#### Discussion of Results

The revised analysis produced an overall SUS mean of 94.00 out of 100, with a sample SD of 5.76. Respondent scores ranged from 87.50 to 100.00. Taken together with the item response patterns and written comments, these results support a strongly favorable overall assessment of perceived usability within the participating pilot group.

The positively worded statements obtained means ranging from 4.60 to 4.80. Respondents expressed favorable views of ease of use, integration of functions, and quick learning, each with a mean of 4.80 and an SD of 0.45. Interest in frequent use and confidence in using the system each received a mean of 4.60 and an SD of 0.55. These response patterns are consistent with the participants' positive impressions of the kiosk and dashboard experience.

After the approved corrections, items 2 and 6 each received a mean of 1.00 and an SD of 0.00, indicating strong disagreement with statements about unnecessary complexity and inconsistency. Items 8 and 10 each received a mean of 1.20 and an SD of 0.45, indicating disagreement with statements about cumbersome use and needing substantial initial learning. Item 4 received a mean of 1.60 and an SD of 0.55, indicating that participants generally disagreed with needing a technical person's support to use the system. These corrected response patterns support favorable impressions of consistency, manageable operation, and confidence in getting started.

#### Conclusion of Usability

The revised SUS results indicate strongly favorable perceived usability among the five pilot evaluators. The mean score of 94.00 out of 100 is supported by positive responses concerning ease of use, integration, confidence, and quick learning, together with the corrected responses for negatively worded items 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10. These findings support a positive overall usability assessment and continued evaluation of BananaVision within the participating business setting.

### Qualitative Feedback

Four of the five evaluators provided optional comments. Their feedback expressed appreciation for the system's convenience, described potential benefits for business efficiency and accuracy, and suggested a conveyor belt as a future enhancement. These comments provide favorable context for the usability results and identify a possible direction for future development.

The conveyor-belt suggestion is consistent with the distinction between the current stationary, manually loaded prototype and a future automated handling system. It represents a proposed enhancement rather than an implemented feature. The comments describe user impressions and expected benefits; they do not independently measure improvements in processing speed or classification accuracy.

## Overall Conclusion of Acceptance Testing

The acceptance evaluation yielded strongly favorable pilot feedback on BananaVision's perceived usability. Following the approved corrections to negatively worded items 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10, the five participants obtained a mean SUS score of 94.00 out of 100 and a sample SD of 5.76. The response patterns and optional comments highlight ease of use, integrated functions, confidence, and convenience as positive aspects of the system.

The results support a positive usability assessment within the participating group and provide a basis for continued evaluation in the intended business setting. The findings remain limited to five evaluators from one wholesale enterprise. Further evaluation involving additional participants and enterprises would strengthen broader conclusions and establish whether the favorable pilot response patterns extend to a wider user population.
